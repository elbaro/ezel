1. Zoom / Pan ​

This only requires updating an uniform variable. There is no wgpu::Buffer update.

The most expensive update is to update ticks and find new nice numbers.

Challenge: Ease-out should happen in transformed or pixel space, not data space (e.g. log scale)

2. Start Animation ​

A data update from initial state (None) will auto-trigger the startup animation.

Example ideas:

  • bar: starts from baseline
  • band: progressive reveal
  • marker: combine position, size, alpha in 800ms-1s
  • A line size starts from 0
  • An alpha starts from 0

3. Data Update ​

  • per-vertex: (old: f32) to (new: f32), from (start_t: f32) to (end_t: f32)
  • uniform: (current_t: f32)

The mouse scroll only needs easing-out but touch interaction (pinch zoom, elastic scroll) requires spring physics to simulate the velocity and acceleration.

Velocity is preserved when starting new animation during another animation.

AI Survey: transition ​

Heer & Robertson (2007) — Animated Transitions in Statistical Data Graphics ​

IEEE Transactions on Visualization and Computer Graphics (Proc. InfoVis), 13(6), 1240–1247

The foundational study on animated transitions between statistical data graphics (bar charts, pie charts, scatter plots). Two controlled experiments found:

  • Animated transitions significantly improved graphical perception over static transitions at both the syntactic (object tracking) and semantic (change estimation) levels of analysis.
  • Simple transitions (~1 second, e.g. translation) outperformed complex ones (e.g. rotation).
  • Staged animation — breaking a complex transition into sequential simpler steps — was preferred over simultaneous animation, which was preferred over no animation at all.
  • For scatter plots specifically, no significant accuracy difference was found between staged and simultaneous animation.

Chevalier, Dragicevic & Franconeri (2014) — The Not-so-Staggering Effect of Staggered Animations on Visual Tracking ​

IEEE Transactions on Visualization and Computer Graphics, 2014

Examines whether staggering (incremental delay in element start times) improves tracking performance:

  • Staggering had a negligible or even negative impact on multiple object tracking performance.
  • Despite the intuition that staggering reduces visual overload and occlusion, empirical evidence did not support this.
  • The effect held across different dot-cloud / scatter-plot-like scenarios.

Kim & Heer (2021) — Gemini: A Grammar and Recommender System for Animated Transitions ​

IEEE Transactions on Visualization and Computer Graphics, 2021

Formalizes animated transitions into a declarative grammar decomposing them into enter, exit, update steps for marks, axes, and legends. The cost function for ranking animation designs was informed by perceptual studies and prefers:

  • Transitions that maintain object constancy (a mark representing the same data point is visually trackable through the animation).
  • Staged over simultaneous transitions when multiple visual properties change.

Paper ·
arXiv

Huth, Blascheck, Koch & Ertl (2023) — Studies and Design Considerations for Animated Transitions Between Small-Scale Visualizations ​

Journal of Visualization, August 2023

Two online studies on animated transitions in small-scale contexts (mobile, embedded in text, geographical overlays):

  • Animated transitions are understandable at small scale, but effectiveness depends heavily on the specific task and operation type.
  • Design considerations differ from normal-sized visualizations.

Paper

Robertson et al. — Animation for Visualization (Book Chapter) ​

Microsoft Research, Chapter 19 in Beautiful Visualization

Provides design principles for visualization animation:

  • Maximize predictability — if the viewer can predict where an element is going after seeing a fraction of the trajectory, cognitive load drops.
  • Slow-in, slow-out easing feels natural and aids tracking.
  • Gestalt common fate — elements that move together are perceived as related (grouped).

AI Survey: ease function ​

No study directly compares fade-in vs. grow vs. fly-in as entrance animations in a controlled experiment. However, the principles above provide strong guidance.

Comparison of Techniques ​

TechniqueDescriptionProsCons
Alpha (fade in)Opacity interpolates from 0 to 1Low cognitive load; does not imply spatial meaning; bubble size is immediately visible and accurateNo object constancy benefit — element "appears" rather than "arrives"
Position (fly from center)Bubble moves from a shared origin to its destinationLeverages object constancy; viewers can track trajectory; communicates spatial placementCan cause crowding/occlusion when many bubbles move simultaneously; staggering does not reliably help (Chevalier et al. 2014)
Size (grow)Radius interpolates from 0 to final valueNaturally draws attention to the size-encoded variable; smooth, focused revealMay mislead viewers into thinking size changed rather than was introduced; briefly misrepresents the data value

Recommendations from the Literature ​

  1. Combine properties. The research (Heer & Robertson 2007; Kim & Heer 2021) suggests interpolating position, size, and opacity simultaneously over a short duration (~800ms–1s) with ease-in-out timing. This is essentially what D3's enter() transitions and Gemini's grammar recommend — bringing elements in with all their visual properties at once to maintain object constancy and avoid implying non-existent data changes.

  2. Keep it short and simple. Transitions around 1 second with simple motion (translation) outperformed longer or more complex ones.

  3. Avoid staggering for tracking tasks. If the goal is for viewers to track individual bubbles, staggering start times does not help and may hurt performance.

  4. Use easing. Slow-in, slow-out (ease-in-out) timing feels natural and aids visual tracking.

  5. If choosing a single technique:

    • Position from center is most aligned with the object constancy principle.
    • Alpha fade is the safest choice that will not accidentally mislead about the data.
    • Size grow is the riskiest — it temporarily shows incorrect data values during the animation.
